TRL1 – Scientific Principles and Research Evidence

1. Objective

Identification and documentation of the fundamental scientific principles supporting automated geometric quality assessment of welded joints.

The research establishes a foundation for subsequent point-cloud-based defect evaluation and Digital Twin development.

2. Scientific background

The fundamental research hypothesis is that geometrical deviations of welded workpieces can be investigated by comparing measured three-dimensional surfaces against suitable reference geometry.

The approach includes:

Three-dimensional representation of welded workpieces.

Reference-based geometric comparison.

Point cloud alignment and spatial deviation analysis.

Identification of measurable surface imperfections.

Assessment of the limitations of geometric measurements.

3. Original research evidence

The initial research is supported by the following publication:

Hungarian Journal of Industry and Chemistry (2021)

DOI: https://doi.org/10.33927/hjic-2021-13

The publication provides methodological and experimental background for geometrical comparison and point-cloud registration.

For TRL1 purposes, the scientific principles and underlying research questions are distinguished from the later experimental implementation.

4. Research hypotheses

H1 – Reference-based point cloud comparison can reveal geometrical deviations associated with welding imperfections.

H2 – Spatial distributions of surface deviations can provide useful information for automated quality assessment.

H3 – Three-dimensional geometric evaluation can complement conventional welding inspection methods.

5. Scope and limitations

Surface-based geometric measurements cannot independently establish the absence of internal weld defects.

The detection of cracks and small-scale imperfections depends on measurement resolution, surface characteristics and processing methodology.

No real-time industrial Digital Twin functionality is claimed at TRL1.

6. Evidence status

The fundamental research principles are retrospectively supported by the scientific publication and historical research documentation.

A separate, contemporaneous TRL1 acceptance record has not yet been verified.

7. Transition towards TRL2

The scientific principles provide the foundation for:

Definition of functional requirements.

Selection of geometric processing methods.

Development of a modular system architecture.

Identification of required measurement inputs and computational outputs.
